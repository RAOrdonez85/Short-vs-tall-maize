# Short vs Tall Maize

An interactive teaching model comparing short-stature (brachytic, *br2*) and conventional tall maize, above and below ground, built from field data measured at Purdue research farms (2018, 2019, 2024 and 2025) under different nitrogen rates.

**Open the interactive page:** use the website link in the *About* section of this repository.

## What students can explore

- Two plants in adjacent rows 76 cm apart for each stature, from the tassel down to 120 cm deep (the depth that was sampled)
- Day after planting from emergence to maturity, with growth stages (V5 … VT, R1 … R6)
- Site (ACRE, SEPAC or all sites) and nitrogen rate (0–300 kg N ha⁻¹)
- Root length density in each soil layer (0–15, 15–30, 30–60, 60–90, 90–120 cm) and when roots from neighboring rows meet in the interrow
- Root length and root biomass over the season
- Plant height and stem elongation rate: internodes elongate in sequence, and *br2* shortens the internodes below the ear
- Ear development: elongation before silking, about 1,200 florets, kernel set during the lag phase, grain fill from blister to maturity, husk and silks
- Leaf senescence, brace roots, tassel and pollen shed
- A model built on the Corn Roots by N Rate model, grounded in unpublished, season-long field measurements across several nitrogen rates

## Collaboration

**Built on original field research.** This model builds on the Corn Roots by N Rate model, which is grounded in unpublished field data: real plant and root measurements taken at multiple points across the growing season, under several nitrogen rates. The short- and tall-stature plants here use that model's structure and parameters for how roots and shoots develop over time, combined with field measurements of each stature. The result captures how plants actually develop through the season, not just how they look at a single sampling date.

**Beyond maize.** The same approach can be applied to other grain crops and legumes. It provides a foundation for studying plant processes and developmental changes more precisely, and for strengthening digital twin platforms focused on plant phenotypes, where models must reproduce real, measured plant behavior.

**Let's collaborate.** Dr. Raziel A. Ordóñez, Plant Scientist and Root Biologist, welcomes partnerships to extend this work to new crops, hybrids, environments and management practices, and to turn field data into tools for research, teaching and decision making.
